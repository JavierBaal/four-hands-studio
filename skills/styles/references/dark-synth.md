# Dark synth and cyberpunk

Analog-style synthesizers, sequenced pulses and a night city: dystopia, technology, neon, rain. Synthwave is the bright, nostalgic variant of the same palette.

## What defines it

- **The sequence is the heartbeat.** 16th-note bass sequences or arpeggios on one or two notes, often with octave or fifth leaps, steady and hypnotic.
- **Minor and modal colour.** Aeolian, Dorian (dark but with a lift), Phrygian for menace. Progressions are slow, often i–bVI–bVII or a pedal with moving upper chords.
- **Big contrast of registers.** A sub bass at the bottom, a cold lead or bell at the top, pads in between.
- **Space.** Long reverbs and delays: notes are short, the space around them is long.
- **Tempo:** dark synth 80–110 BPM, heavier and slower; driving cyberpunk 110–130; synthwave 90–120 with a straight backbeat.
- **Drums:** electronic, simple: kick on the beat or half-time, a big snare or clap on 3 (half-time) or 2 and 4, metallic hats. In darker cues, drums can be just a pulse or absent.

## Typical moves in Four Hands

- **Bass sequence:** one bar of 16ths in `add_notes` (e.g. root–root–octave–root with accents every 3), then copy and change one note every two bars to follow the harmony. Vary velocities so the "filter" feels alive.
- **Arpeggio layer:** 16th or triplet arpeggios of the chord in the G3–G5 band, quieter than the bass, out of the lead's octave.
- **Pads:** long chords in open voicing, entering slowly (low velocity first, growing).
- **Lead:** a simple, slow melody high up, with long notes; answer it with silence. Sparse is better.
- **Tension device:** keep the bass on the tonic while the harmony above moves to bII or bVI; release by moving the bass at last.
- **Build:** add one layer every 4 or 8 bars; drop the drums for a bar before the peak.

## Clichés to avoid

- **Generic 80s pop:** bright major chords, gated snare on every 2 and 4 without variation, saxophone solo. Only if the user wants synthwave nostalgia.
- **EDM build-drop:** risers and drops every 8 bars. This style stays hypnotic.
- **Everything at 16ths:** if bass, arp and drums all run 16ths in the same register, it becomes noise. Give each layer a different rate or register.
- **Orchestral cinema by default:** strings and horns can colour it, but the core is synths.

## Sound

- **With built-in sounds:** GM synth bass, saw and square leads, pads (warm, polysynth, halo), synth brass for stabs, an electronic drum kit. They are placeholders.
- **For the user's libraries (annotate):** bass → Vital or Serum, saw or square with low-pass filter and envelope; arps → plucky patch with delay; pads → wide detuned saws, slow attack; lead → mono with glide; drums → an 80s electronic kit or processed samples.
